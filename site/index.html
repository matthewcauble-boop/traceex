01 · The fix

The model said 9:35 AM8:05 AM.
Your checker fixed it.
Now you own that.

A checker doesn't need to know the right answer. It only has to prove which field is wrong, using rules grounded in the input. When the fix passes, it's the most valuable training signal there is, and it becomes a trace with your wallet on it.

What leaves your device

Every value is swapped for a typed placeholder on your machine, the same one wherever it appears. The key that maps them back never leaves. Trainers buy the shape of the mistake, never the person.

02 · Sorted and searchable

Every trace is filed, and every mistake has a name.

The classifier places each trace in a task taxonomy (TypeSafe Jev hierarchical search, with an offline fallback) and reads exactly how the model failed straight from the placeholders. Search ranks the best match first; pick a branch or a failure mode to narrow it, or sort by what feeds an open bounty.

Share a verified fix

Paste what the model saw, what it said, and what your checker proved right. Personal text becomes a skeleton in your browser before anything is saved, and keys or tokens are refused.

The coin, live

      
Contributors earn TXC. Users pay dollars, which buy TXC from the pool, and half of what they buy is burned. Nobody sets the price; the pool does.

      

        
$0.0100per TXC

        
1,000,000TXC in existence

        
0burned by usage and fees

        
0vesting to contributors

        
0validators

      

      
    
        
  1. Paid for proof, not volume. TXC is minted only when validators find a learning better on data they each hold privately. A trace earns only through a learning that passed.
    2. 
        
  2. Validators you can't pick. They are drawn at random after you submit, commit their scores before anyone reveals, and the median decides, so one bought validator can't move it.
    3. 
        
  3. Skin in the game. Trainers bond, validators stake, rewards vest for four epochs, and anyone can challenge. Fresh validators re-measure, and a fake is clawed back.
    4. 
        
  4. Paying yourself loses. Every payment burns half of what it buys, and usage can mint at most half of what it burned.
    5. 
        
  5. Copies and padding earn nothing. Near-duplicates pay the original producer. Validators audit parents, and padded ones lose their share.
    6. 
      

      
Every strategy in the attack simulation loses money against honest work, except owning most of the validator stake, which is the limit of any proof-of-stake network.

A verified fix is exactly the training example open models lack: a real input, the model's real failure, and an answer a checker proved right. 08 · On autopilot

    
Agents use it without being told to.

    
Connect an agent once. From then on it submits every verified fix, searches the exchange when it keeps failing, adopts learnings that proved themselves, and posts a bounty, free, when a failure keeps coming back that nothing fixes. It only spends what its owner allows.

# or in code: any agent built on the SDK

Five lines.
Your fixes start earning.

    

      
    
        
  1. Keep your loop
    Any model, any task. If you already extract and check, AdaptiveAgent wraps it and emits traces as fixes happen.
    2. 
        
  2. Submit traces
    Each one is turned into a skeleton on your machine, classified, and filed under your wallet.
    3. 
        
  3. Back or post bounties
    Post the problem you need solved for free, or buy into one you believe in.
    4. 
        
  4. Adopt what's proven
    agent.adopt(learning) applies a learning that passed a validator's hidden eval.
